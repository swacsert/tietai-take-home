# 1b · Tactical plan — San Andrés → OMOP

**Working assumptions** (each confirmed by a pre-kickoff question in `questions.md`): the first cohort is oncology (Q1); the DPO accepts pseudonymisation at the edge (Q2); Marta can deliver the edge host — VM or physical server — within 2 weeks (Q3); Diego gives ~30% of his time (Q6); the HIS can export the cohort's history window as a batch (Q1, Q7).

## 1. Phasing — what ships

Each phase is defined by what the hospital can see and use, not by our tasks.

| | **Week 2 — "We see your real data, nothing leaves"** | **Week 6 — "Real data in pre-production OMOP"** | **Week 12 — "Validated first cohort in production, Diego runs it"** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ships** | Hydra Connect installed on the edge host. Channel 1 reading the HIS drop folder **locally**. **Profiling report** of real data: volume per hour, half-written and repeated files, Z-segment catalogue, identifiers per source, CIE-10-ES code frequencies, 50 sample oncology `Condition`s. **Signed cohort definition.** DPO decision | HIS + oncology FHIR → Person, Visit_occurrence, Condition_occurrence in a **pre-production** OMOP schema. Edge pseudonymisation with field allow-list and identifier scanner. Patient identity resolution. CIE-10-ES → SNOMED for the codes covering 90% of occurrences. Idempotent loader, local DLQ, "HIS stopped writing" alert. Read-only access for Dr. Ruiz and two researchers | Measurement (labs → LOINC). Data-quality checks. **Week-8 go/no-go → production.** History for the cohort window only. Cohort validated by Dr. Ruiz in week 11, delivered in week 12 with a reconciliation report (received vs loaded). Diego passes the handover exit test. openEHR mapping reviewed by Dr. Ruiz, with an estimate to load it |
| **Deliberately not yet** | No data leaves the hospital: the 443 rule may be late (R5) and pseudonymisation is not validated yet (R4). Nothing loaded into OMOP | Nothing in production (decision 3). No Measurement, no openEHR, no history | No openEHR load unless the cohort is chronic (decision 1). No history outside the cohort window. No MLLP, no real time, no second cohort, no researcher self-service |
| **Decision point** | End of week 2: the cohort decides the sources. Diabetes or cardiology → openEHR swaps in for oncology | **Week 4:** < 95% of oncology patients matched to the HIS (R1) → fix the identifier before going further | Week 8: go/no-go to production. Week 11: Dr. Ruiz's sign-off and Diego's exit test. If either fails, nothing ships just because of the date |

**Why Measurement waits until weeks 7–12:** for an oncology cohort the diagnosis matters more than labs, so Condition first protects week 6. A diabetes cohort reverses this.

## 2. Top 5 risks

| Risk | P × I | Owner | Mitigation | Trigger |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **R1 · Technical.** **Edge pseudonymisation breaks patient linkage.** Each source is hashed separately; if HIS and oncology use different identifiers, the same patient becomes two Persons. HIS merges (ADT^A40) change identifiers after load | Medium × High | TietAI lead (Dr. Ruiz reviews doubtful matches) | Week-2 profiling lists the identifiers of each source; hash one shared, normalised field in all channels; A40 handled as a merge, never a new patient; doubtful matches to the review queue — a false merge is worse than a duplicate | Week 4: < 95% of oncology patients matched to an HIS visit, or any A40 with no handling |
| **R2 · Operational.** **The HIS stops writing files and nobody notices.** No ACK: a moved path, a full disk or an expired service-account password raises no error — the gap shows up weeks later as a missing month | High × Medium | Diego (Marta for disk and service account) | Expected volume per hour from profiling; alert at < 30% of expected for 60 min; disk alert at 80%; password rotation in Marta's calendar; daily reconciliation of files written vs accepted + DLQ | 0 files for > 60 min between 08:00 and 20:00, or daily volume < 50% of that weekday's average |
| **R3 · Clinical.** **Same tumour, two vocabularies.** HIS codes CIE-10-ES; oncology FHIR may use ICD-O-3, local codes or free text — two concepts for one cancer, or `concept_id = 0` | Medium-High × High | Dr. Ruiz | 50 sample oncology `Condition`s in week 2; a written source-of-truth rule per domain signed by Dr. Ruiz (e.g. cancer from oncology, comorbidities from HIS); map by frequency; "`concept_id = 0` by source" report on every load | Week 6: > 5% of cohort diagnoses unmapped, or > 10% of oncology patients with one diagnosis under two concepts |
| **R4 · Compliance.** **Identifiers leak outside the pseudonymised fields** — a DNI, phone or name in free text, Z-segments, FHIR `Patient.contact` or openEHR narrative. One leak breaks the basis on which the DPO approved the design | Medium × High | TietAI lead (hospital DPO approves) | Allow-list at the edge: only mapped fields leave; Z-segments and free text stay in the edge store until Dr. Ruiz classifies them; pattern scanner (DNI/NIE, phone, email) blocks the event to the local DLQ; agreed key-rotation procedure | Any DNI/NIE or phone pattern outside PID, in profiling or any later load |
| **R5 · Organizational.** **Edge host and outbound rule not ready for week 2.** The whole critical path starts there, and a request that just misses Marta's change window can turn week 2 into week 5 | Medium-High × High | Marta | One complete written change request in week 0 (host size and OS, FQDN, 443, proxy/TLS inspection, read access to the drop folder); plan against her next window; week-2 profiling runs locally and needs no outbound rule | Day 5 without the edge host, or day 10 without the 443 rule approved |

## 3. User empathy

Every request has a time and a moment; every protection is something we will **not** ask; every "in return" removes work.

| | **Marta** — IT lead, sceptical of vendors | **Dr. Ruiz** — clinical informaticist, burned before | **Diego** — junior data engineer, future owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Weeks 0–2** | One request list for one change window, reviewed with her first (45 min). ≈ 4 h | Profiling review (1 h), cohort definition (1 h), classify Z-segments (1 h), "known traps" (30 min). ≈ 3.5 h | **Observe** (≈ 4 h/week): pairs on the install and profiling. Starts the runbook |
| **Weeks 2–6** | One-page report every 2 weeks. ≈ 30 min/week | Fixed 90-min Thursday slot: source-of-truth rule, codes covering 90%, all doubtful, a 5% sample of automatic ones. ≈ 6 h | **Co-pilot** (≈ 8 h/week): he types, we watch. First guided DLQ replay; writes the replay procedure |
| **Weeks 6–12** | Approves production in one window, week 8 (≈ 2 h). Freeze in weeks 10–12 | Quality thresholds, 50-patient go/no-go sample, two openEHR sessions, cohort validation in week 11, review queue. ≈ 15 h | **Operate with support** (≈ 10–12 h/week): first to answer alerts in working hours; adds a lab mapping alone in week 8 |
| **Shielded from** | Inbound ports, VPN, domain-admin accounts, piecemeal requests, debugging channels, being first responder | Reading raw HL7, validating exact mappings one by one, discovering errors after delivery | On call, platform internals, clinical mapping decisions, asking Marta for changes alone |
| **In return** | A security sheet she can verify, and a **kill switch**: stopping the edge service cuts the flow without touching the HIS | Each decision applied once and versioned with his name; an **explicit veto** — no week-11 sign-off, no cohort | A runbook he wrote, support within 1 h until week 12, then 30 days of weekly hypercare and a named TietAI contact |
| **After week 12** | 30 min per quarter | 30–45 min every 2 weeks on new mappings | **Operates alone** as first line (≈ 3–4 h/week); TietAI is second line |

**Leaving Diego able to run it alone.** Handover is declared by a test, not a date. In week 11 we inject into pre-production a half-written HL7 file, a malformed Z-segment and a 60-minute gap in HIS files. Alone, in under 2 hours, Diego must notice it from the alert, find the cause in the DLQ, fix or route it (mapping → Dr. Ruiz), replay end to end, show zero duplicates in OMOP, and write a five-line incident note. If he cannot, we repeat in week 12 and extend support by two weeks.

**Total effort over 12 weeks:** Marta ≈ 10 h + 2 change windows · Dr. Ruiz ≈ 25 h (peak 4 h in week 11) · Diego ≈ 100 h, ramping up.

## 4. Decision log — three things we would push back on

| Push-back | Their position | Our position | What we table |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 · Scope** | *Research lead / Dr. Ruiz:* all three sources, openEHR included, in the week-12 cohort — a unified view is what they signed for, and they have heard "in phase 2" before | openEHR is not one more connector: templates, archetypes, units and AQL must be analysed with a third vendor first. Three sources in 12 weeks likely ends as three half-done sources and a cohort Dr. Ruiz cannot validate | HIS + oncology to week 12; two 90-min openEHR template sessions and one tested read-only AQL query in weeks 8–11, so week 12 includes the reviewed openEHR mapping and an estimate to load it *[assumption: weeks 13–18]*. **Swap clause:** a diabetes or cardiology cohort puts openEHR in and moves oncology out |
| **2 · Tool** | *Marta:* no new vendor machine on her network — patching, AD, monitoring and the risk are hers | With no public ingress and pseudonymisation required before data leaves, something of ours must run inside. Without it, identifiers leave the hospital or the hash lives in a component nobody tests, and Diego loses the local DLQ and replay | A security sheet in week 1 she checks herself: hardened image, **zero inbound ports**, one outbound 443 to a fixed host, read-only service account. Installed in one change window in week 2, with us connected; we prepare the patches. VM or server, her choice |
| **3 · Deadline** | *Research lead (and Marta):* if real data loads in week 6, open production then and gain a month; another environment is one more thing to maintain | First loads **will** carry identity and mapping errors. Once a researcher extracts wrong data, it cannot be recalled — and Dr. Ruiz's trust is lost in week 6. Pseudonymisation protects privacy, not accuracy | Pre-production is a separate OMOP schema on the same server, not a new environment. Real data for Dr. Ruiz and two researchers from week 6; production in week 8 against written criteria *[to negotiate]*: ≥ 95% of cohort codes mapped, 0 open critical issues, doubtful matches < 1% and reviewed, 50 patients checked by Dr. Ruiz against the HIS |
